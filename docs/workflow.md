# Research and development workflow

This loop combines progress on a project with opportunities to learn. Steps may be repeated, shortened, skipped, or performed in a different order.

## 1. Orient

Agree on the problem, who it matters to, the desired artifact, the user's learning interests, and practical constraints. Ask what the user already knows only to the extent useful for the work.

**Capture:** a short, editable project brief.

## 2. Map the problem

Identify key concepts, assumptions, unknowns, possible approaches, and risks. Distinguish questions answerable by evidence from those requiring preference or judgment.

**Capture:** a provisional map and a manageable next question.

## 3. Explore

Investigate through reading, discussion, observation, prototypes, or other appropriate methods. The user chooses how much help to receive. A prediction before an experiment can be useful, but is optional.

**Capture:** relevant notes, sources, hypotheses, and unanswered questions.

## 4. Compare perspectives

When valuable, ask different models, roles, or people to review a focused question. Separate evidence-backed claims from opinions and unresolved disagreements. See [Multi-model review](multi-model-review.md).

**Capture:** agreements, disagreements, supporting evidence, and uncertainties.

## 5. Decide and experiment

The user selects a direction. Define a small next step, what it is intended to test or accomplish, and—where applicable—what outcomes would support or challenge the idea.

**Capture:** the decision, alternatives considered, reasons, and expected observations.

## 6. Build and review

Do the research or development work. Review artifacts and results against the stated goals. Link important claims to sources and important choices to their rationale where practical.

**Capture:** artifacts, test or observation results, and known limitations.

## 7. Reflect and continue

At a natural stopping point, offer a brief review: What changed in the project? What did the user learn or practice? What remains uncertain? What is the next useful step? The user can skip or edit it.

**Capture:** a concise summary useful for resuming the work.

## Minimal project brief

```text
Project / question:
Why it matters:
Desired artifact or decision:
What I want to learn or practice:
What I already know (optional):
Constraints and sensitivities:
Preferred assistance mode:
Next small step:
```

The brief is a working document, not a form that must be completed before starting.
